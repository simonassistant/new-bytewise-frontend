# Academic Integrity and AI Citation Guidelines for UE1

## Core Principle: AI Tools ≠ Academic Sources

**Fundamental Rule**: AI-generated content cannot be cited as an academic source in scholarly work.

## Why AI Cannot Be Cited as a Source

### **1. Lack of Authority and Expertise**
- AI models lack credentials, qualifications, or expertise
- No peer review process for AI-generated content
- Cannot be held accountable for accuracy or claims

### **2. No Verifiable Origin**
- AI responses synthesize from multiple unknown sources
- Cannot trace information back to original research
- No publication date, author, or institutional affiliation

### **3. Reproducibility Issues**
- AI responses can vary for identical queries
- No permanent, citable location for the information
- Content may change or become unavailable

## Proper AI Documentation Methods

### **Method 1: Methodology Documentation (Recommended)**

**In Academic Papers:**
```
In the methodology section or author's note:
"AI assistance was used for [specific purpose, e.g., brainstorming, 
grammar checking, outline organization] using [tool name] on [date]. 
All ideas, arguments, and analysis remain the original work of the author."
```

**Example:**
> "ChatGPT was used on September 18, 2025, to help clarify complex terminology and suggest organizational structures for this essay. All arguments, evidence selection, and analysis are original to the author."

### **Method 2: Acknowledgment Section**

**For Research Projects:**
```
ACKNOWLEDGMENTS
I acknowledge the use of [AI tool] to assist with [specific tasks] 
during the [research/writing] process. All content, analysis, and 
conclusions are my original work, verified through credible academic sources.
```

### **Method 3: Process Portfolio Documentation**

**For UE1 Assignments:**
- Document AI interactions in writing process portfolio
- Explain how AI assistance enhanced learning
- Show verification of AI-provided information
- Demonstrate original thinking and analysis

## What to Document About AI Use

### **Required Information:**
1. **Tool Used**: Specific AI platform (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)
2. **Date of Use**: When AI assistance was used
3. **Purpose**: Specific reason for AI assistance
4. **Scope**: What aspects of work involved AI
5. **Verification**: How information was confirmed

### **Documentation Template:**
```
AI ASSISTANCE DISCLOSURE

Tool: [Name of AI tool]
Date: [Date of use]
Purpose: [Specific reason - e.g., "grammar feedback on draft"]
Scope: [What was AI-assisted - e.g., "sentence structure suggestions"]
Verification: [How accuracy was confirmed - e.g., "checked grammar rules in style guide"]
Original Work: [Confirmation that ideas/analysis are student's own]
```

## Academic Source Standards for UE1

### **Acceptable Sources:**
1. **Peer-reviewed academic journals**
2. **Scholarly books from university presses**
3. **Reputable news organizations** (for current events)
4. **Government and institutional reports**
5. **Primary sources** (interviews, surveys, documents)

### **How to Verify AI Information:**
1. **Cross-reference with credible sources**
2. **Check claims against academic databases**
3. **Consult subject matter experts**
4. **Use fact-checking resources**
5. **Apply critical thinking skills**

## AI-Assisted Research Process for UE1

### **Step 1: Initial Exploration**
- Use AI to understand complex topics
- Generate research questions
- Explore different perspectives
- **Document**: "Used AI to explore research directions"

### **Step 2: Source Finding**
- Ask AI for guidance on where to look for sources
- Get suggestions for search terms
- Understand research methodologies
- **Document**: "AI provided research strategy guidance"

### **Step 3: Source Verification**
- Use AI to help understand difficult texts
- Get clarification on technical terms
- Check understanding of concepts
- **Document**: "AI assisted with reading comprehension"

### **Step 4: Writing Process**
- AI feedback on drafts
- Grammar and style suggestions
- Organizational advice
- **Document**: "AI provided writing feedback and editing suggestions"

## Common AI Use Scenarios in UE1

### **Scenario 1: Understanding Complex Readings**
**Appropriate**: "Help me understand this paragraph from [academic source]"
**Documentation**: "AI assisted with comprehension of course readings"
**Citation**: Cite the original academic source, not AI

### **Scenario 2: Grammar and Style Help**
**Appropriate**: "Review this paragraph for grammar and clarity"
**Documentation**: "AI provided grammar and style feedback"
**Citation**: No citation needed (similar to spell-check)

### **Scenario 3: Research Direction**
**Appropriate**: "What are key themes in research about [topic]?"
**Documentation**: "AI helped identify research themes"
**Citation**: Find and cite actual research sources, not AI response

### **Scenario 4: Brainstorming**
**Appropriate**: "Help me think of different angles for [assignment]"
**Documentation**: "AI assisted with brainstorming and idea generation"
**Citation**: No citation needed for brainstorming assistance

## Red Flags: Inappropriate AI Use

### **Never Cite AI For:**
- Factual claims or statistics
- Expert opinions or analysis
- Historical information
- Scientific data
- Literary interpretation
- Research findings

### **Instead, Use AI To:**
- Find where to look for credible sources
- Understand how to evaluate sources
- Get clarification on confusing concepts
- Improve writing clarity and organization

## UE1 Assignment-Specific Guidelines

### **Research Essays:**
- AI can help develop research questions
- AI cannot provide research findings
- Must cite credible academic sources for all claims
- Document AI assistance in methodology

### **Literary Analysis:**
- AI can help understand difficult passages
- AI cannot provide interpretations to cite
- Must develop original analysis
- Support interpretations with textual evidence

### **Argumentative Essays:**
- AI can help organize arguments
- AI cannot provide evidence or expert opinions
- Must research and cite credible sources
- Document any organizational assistance

## Faculty Guidelines for UE1

### **Clear Policies:**
1. **Explain AI vs. source distinction** in syllabi
2. **Provide documentation templates** for students
3. **Model appropriate AI use** in class
4. **Require source verification** for all claims

### **Assessment Strategies:**
1. **Process portfolios** showing AI use documentation
2. **Source analysis assignments** to build evaluation skills
3. **In-class discussions** about AI responses and credibility
4. **Revision exercises** focusing on source integration

## Quick Reference: AI Documentation Checklist

**Before Using AI:**
- [ ] Is this an appropriate use for my assignment?
- [ ] How will I verify any information provided?
- [ ] What documentation will I need to keep?

**During AI Use:**
- [ ] Am I asking for assistance, not content generation?
- [ ] Am I maintaining my original thinking and analysis?
- [ ] Am I planning to verify information independently?

**After AI Use:**
- [ ] Have I verified information with credible sources?
- [ ] Have I documented the AI assistance appropriately?
- [ ] Are all my citations to credible academic sources?
- [ ] Is my original thinking clearly evident in the work?

---

**Remember**: AI is a tool to enhance learning, not replace it. Your ideas, analysis, and critical thinking are what make your academic work valuable and original.
